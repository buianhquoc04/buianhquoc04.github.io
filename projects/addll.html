HIGH-SPEED DIGITAL IC DESIGN

Fast-Lock All-Digital Delay-Locked Loop

A 45 nm All-Digital Delay-Locked Loop based on a two-step Time-to-Digital Converter, designed for fast phase locking and clock-skew compensation across a wide operating frequency range.

GPDK 45 nm ADDLL 2-Step TDC DCDL PVT Analysis High-Speed Simulation
PROJECT OVERVIEW

Fast phase acquisition using a two-step TDC architecture

The project focuses on the design and simulation of a high-speed All-Digital Delay-Locked Loop implemented in a 45 nm CMOS process.

The proposed architecture combines a Digitally Controlled Delay Line with a two-step Time-to-Digital Converter consisting of coarse and fine delay-search stages. After the initial fast-lock search, the DLL transitions into closed-loop sequential tracking.

PROCESS GPDK 45 nm
FREQUENCY 1.65–7 GHz
LOCKING 6–8 Cycles
VDD 1.1 V
01 — ARCHITECTURE

Proposed ADDLL Architecture

The architecture integrates a Digitally Controlled Delay Line (DCDL), two-step TDC, Timing Controller, Phase Detector, Shift Register Controller, and coarse/fine shift registers. The delayed clock is also passed through replica and clock-distribution buffer paths to compensate for clock-distribution delay.

02 — FAST-LOCK OPERATION

Two-Step TDC Search

During startup, the DLL first performs a coarse TDC search followed by a fine TDC search. The generated coarse and fine delay codes are then stored and applied to the DCDL. After completing the initial search, the TDC is disabled and the system transitions into closed-loop sequential tracking.

COARSE SEARCH 2 Cycles
FINE SEARCH 2 Cycles
CODE STORE 2 Cycles
03 — DCDL DESIGN

Coarse and Fine Delay Control

The DCDL consists of coarse and fine delay stages. The coarse delay path provides large delay adjustment, while the fine delay line enables picosecond-level timing resolution for final phase alignment.

MINIMUM DCDL DELAY ≈ 65.2 ps
CDU DELAY ≈ 39–40 ps
FDU RESOLUTION ≈ 2.5 ps
04 — SIMULATION RESULT

Phase Lock at 1.65 GHz

At the nominal operating condition (VDD = 1.1 V, TT process, 27°C), the ADDLL successfully aligned the output clock with the reference clock.

FREQUENCY 1.65 GHz
LOCK TIME 6 Cycles
POST-LOCK SKEW ≈ 0.8 ps
05 — PVT CHARACTERIZATION

Frequency and PVT Evaluation

The complete ADDLL was characterized across process and temperature conditions at several operating frequencies. The results demonstrate successful high-frequency operation in multiple PVT conditions while also identifying marginal and failing corners that require further delay-line and architecture optimization.

MAX CHARACTERIZED FREQUENCY 7 GHz
POWER @ 1.65 GHz ≈ 400 µW
POWER @ 7 GHz ≈ 1.05 mW
Engineering Note

Operation at 7 GHz was demonstrated under multiple PVT conditions; however, not every extreme corner achieved the same locking quality. These results were used to identify the DCDL and fine-delay resolution as key areas for further optimization.

The next stage of the project focuses on physical implementation of the DCDL and further optimization of the delay architecture to improve robustness at high-frequency and extreme PVT corners.
